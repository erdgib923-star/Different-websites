Small Rules

Eight drawings by eight short programs, hung along one corridor.

Every work on these walls was made by a rule you could write on a postcard. Walk the corridor by scrolling. Select any frame to see the work larger, with its rule.

  1. No. 1

    Drift Lines

    Maren Oyelowo-Strand

    2025 · Canvas, 520 strokes

    Each stroke follows the slope of an invisible landscape for forty steps, then lifts.

  2. No. 2

    Room for Everyone

    Teodor Valbrand

    2024 · Canvas, 260 circles

    Drop a circle anywhere and grow it until it touches a neighbour. Repeat until nothing more fits.

  3. No. 3

    Nine by Nine

    Ines Okonkwo-Lund

    2026 · Canvas, 81 tiles

    Every tile holds two quarter-circles, turned one of two ways by a coin toss.

  4. No. 4

    Floor Plan for a Small House

    Arvid Castellanos

    2023 · Canvas, ink and one colour

    Split the longer side somewhere near the middle. Stop at random, or when a room gets too small.

  5. No. 5

    Two Stones, One Pond

    Sun-hee Marchetti

    2025 · Canvas, two ring sets

    Two sets of rings, a little apart. The pattern you see is in neither of them.

  6. No. 6

    Survey of an Imagined Hill

    Elsbeth Narváez

    2026 · Canvas, 30 contours

    Thirty rings, each pushed in or out by the same slow noise. Every sixth one is in colour.

  7. No. 7

    Seven Errands

    Kofi Lindqvist

    2024 · Canvas, 1,820 steps

    Seven walkers start in the centre and take 260 random steps each on a grid. One is in colour.

  8. No. 8

    Moon, Nine Thousand Tries

    Paz Ellery-Moore

    2025 · Canvas, 9,000 attempts

    Throw nine thousand dots. Keep each one with the chance that the sphere is dark at that spot.

Why small

A museum for things you could hold in one hand.

We collect small objects, small books and, this season, small programs. None of the eight rules on show is longer than a few lines, yet each draws something you would not have guessed from reading it.

The works are drawn fresh on your screen each time you visit, from the same rule and the same seed, so you see exactly what the artists saw.
