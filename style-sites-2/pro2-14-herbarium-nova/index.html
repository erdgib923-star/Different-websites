Herbarium Nova

Being a Collection of Plants that Never Were,
Grown by Rule and Drawn Stroke by Stroke

Scroll, and each plate draws itself in the order its plant grows. Numbered labels appear as the pen reaches them.

Plate I

Nova filix Common lantern-fern

A creeping fern of damp limestone. Each frond divides twice, then rests; the old fronds stay on the plant as a brown skirt through winter.

  1. 1Terminal frond, uncurled
  2. 2Lateral node, where the frond divides
  3. 3Rhizome, the creeping root-stem

Grown by rule. X → F+[[X]−X]−F[−FX]+X, F → FF, angle 25°, five generations.

Plate II

Lumen cordata Hearth bush

A low, many-stemmed shrub of sunny banks. It flowers on the outermost tips only, in small vermilion clusters that close at dusk.

Grown by rule. F → FF−[−F+F+F]+[+F−F−F], angle 22.5°, four generations.

Plate III

Sepia arborea Ink tree

A slender tree whose bark yields a brown dye. Each branch splits left, then right, then continues; it is the most orderly plant in the collection.

Grown by rule. X → F[+X]F[−X]+X, F → FF, angle 20°, six generations.

Plate IV

Verdigris spiralis Copper-weed

Named for the blue-green bloom on its leaves, which rubs off on fingers like old copper. Branches in perfect pairs.

Grown by rule. X → F[+X][−X]FX, F → FF, angle 25.7°, six generations.

Preface

Every plant in this book was grown, not found. Each begins as a single letter and a handful of rules: replace this letter with that string, five or six times over. A turtle with a pen then walks the result. F means step forward and draw, + and − mean turn, and the brackets mean remember this place and come back to it.

The method is borrowed from the study of real plant growth, but the species are entirely imaginary, as are their habitats, collectors and dates. If a plate reminds you of something in a hedgerow, that is the rules doing what living things do.

Herbarium Nova is a fictional collection made for a design demo. Every species, specimen number, place and date is invented. Correspondence: curator@example.com · (555) 555-0100.
