Harbor Council Votes to Relight the Old North Lighthouse

After 41 years dark, the keeper’s cottage will reopen as a museum next spring. Residents are split over the cost.

By Mara Ellison, Harbor Desk

The lamp at the top of the Old North Lighthouse has not turned since the autumn of 1985. On Thursday night, by a vote of five to two, the harbor council agreed it should turn again.

The plan, two years in the drafting, restores the lantern room with an electric light that mimics the old rotating beam, repairs the iron gallery and converts the keeper’s cottage into a small museum of the harbor’s fishing history.

“It is the first thing anyone sees from the water,” said council member Ruth Okafor, who proposed the motion. Opponents pointed to the price, which the council’s own estimate puts at $1.8 million spread over three years, and asked whether the money would be better spent on the Pier 4 seawall.

Work is expected to begin in February. If the schedule holds, the light will be switched on for the first time in four decades on the evening of the spring tide in April.

Opinion · The Gazette’s view

A Light Is Worth More Than Its Cost

Lighthouses stopped being necessary long before they stopped being loved. Satellite navigation does the old keeper’s job better than any lamp. So why spend $1.8 million to relight one?

Because a harbor is more than its traffic. The Old North light is on the town seal, the school crest and half the postcards in the chandlery. It is how this town tells strangers who it is.

The seawall matters too, and the council should not let the lighthouse crowd it out of next year’s budget. But a town that only fixes what is broken forgets what it is for. Turn the light back on.
